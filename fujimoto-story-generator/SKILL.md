## Overview

Generates original manga oneshot scripts (15-40 pages) driven by character dynamics — the specific, charged tension between two people. The engine of every Fujimoto story is not plot but relationship: what each character wants from the other, what they can't give, and why they can't let go.

## When to Use

- User asks for a "Fujimoto-style" story or oneshot
- User wants a manga/comic script written
- User wants a short, emotionally raw, cinematic narrative
- User wants a story with surreal elements, tonal shifts, or meta-textual moments

## Core Principle

Character dynamics drive everything. Before any plot, generate:

- **Character A** — their core want (specific, irrational, emotionally honest)
- **Character B** — their core want (complementary or conflicting)
- **The tension** — what A wants from B that B can't or won't give, and why neither can walk away
- **What each represents to the other** — A might represent freedom to B; B might represent validation to A

Document this in 3-4 sentences before writing anything else. If the dynamic isn't compelling, the story won't work.

## Manga Script Format

```
---

PAGE 1

PANEL 1 (wide, establishing)
[composition description — setting, mood, camera distance]

NARRATION:
Character monologue, if any.

PANEL 2 (medium, character focus)
[action description]

CHARACTER: "Dialogue"

---
```

- Each page gets a `PAGE X` header
- Panels numbered within the page
- Camera direction in parentheses: (wide), (medium), (close-up), (extreme close-up), (over-the-shoulder), (low angle), (bird's eye)
- Dialogue follows character name and colon in ALL CAPS
- Narration/voiceover labeled `NARRATION:`

## Generation Workflow

Follow these stages in order. Do not skip ahead.

### Stage 1: Character Dynamic

Given the user's prompt (or prompted to generate from nothing):

1. Propose 2-3 character pair concepts with the dynamic defined for each
2. Ask the user which to develop, or choose if they want you to decide
3. Refine the chosen dynamic into 3-4 sentences covering: each character's want, the specific tension, what each represents

### Stage 2: Emotional Core

Write one sentence: what the story is *really* about.

The emotional core is never the surface premise. A story about two manga artists is about validation and rivalry. A story about a devil hunter is about connection and belonging. A story about a movie theater is about letting go.

### Stage 3: Beat Sheet

Map 5-8 beats across 15-40 pages:

| Beat | Pages | Description |
|------|-------|-------------|
| Ordinary World | 2-4 | Characters introduced through their dynamic in a mundane context |
| Inciting Shift | 2-4 | Something disrupts the equilibrium |
| Escalation | 4-8 | The dynamic deepens; obsession or commitment grows |
| Peak / False Resolution | 3-5 | Apparent climax that feels like an ending |
| Subversive Turn | 2-4 | The real conflict reveals itself; the initial premise is not the actual story |
| Climax | 3-6 | Emotional confrontation — not physical, unless the physical IS emotional |
| Aftermath | 2-4 | Quiet resolution. The story lingers. |

Adjust page counts as needed. The total must land between 15-40 pages.

### Stage 4: Script Writing

Write the full script following the format above. Apply Fujimoto conventions throughout:

**Cinematic paneling:** Mix extreme wide establishing shots with extreme close-ups. "Camera" lingers on mundane details (a half-empty glass, crumpled paper, a scarred hand). Cut between scenes like film edits — no transition, just the next page.

**Tonal whiplash:** Place absurd or darkly comic moments adjacent to devastating emotional beats. A character laughing at something grotesque. A tender moment interrupted by sudden violence. The tonal shift should feel jarring but intentional — it mirrors how life actually feels.

**Subversive climax:** The emotional payoff subverts the story's apparent premise. If the story appears to be about achieving a goal, the climax is about losing something else. If it's about a relationship succeeding, the climax is about understanding why it can't.

**Surreal intrusion:** Reality bends without explanation or foreshadowing. A door opens to somewhere impossible. A character's obsession manifests as a physical presence. An object reappears in impossible places. Never explain it — just show it.

**Meta-awareness (sparing):** Characters occasionally acknowledge they're in a story. A character counts panels. Someone asks "how many pages are left?" Once per oneshot maximum.

**Emotional honesty:** Characters say things people actually say in vulnerable moments — embarrassing, cruel, needy, pathetic. Dialogue is not clever. It is true.

**Obsession-driven protagonists:** The main character's desire is specific and irrational. They don't want what's good for them. They want this one specific thing, person, or outcome, and the story is about what that wanting does to them.

### Stage 5: Self-Check

Verify against checklist before presenting the final script. Fix any issues found.

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Character dynamic is the actual engine of the story (not plot events)
- [ ] Emotional core sentence exists and is true to the final script
- [ ] At least one tonal shift between adjacent pages (humor/tragedy, mundane/surreal)
- [ ] "Camera" choices are intentional — not all default medium shots
- [ ] Surreal or reality-bending element present (even if very subtle)
- [ ] Climax subverts the setup premise in an emotionally truthful way
- [ ] At least one line of dialogue or narration that feels uncomfortably honest
- [ ] Dialogue sounds natural, not expository — people talk around what they mean
- [ ] Ending is quiet and lingers — no tidy resolution, no punchline
- [ ] Total page count is between 15-40
- [ ] Script format is correct (PAGE headers, panel descriptions, dialogue formatting)
- [ ] No exposition explaining the surreal element

## Common Mistakes

- **Plot-driven instead of dynamic-driven** — If the story works with different characters, the dynamic isn't specific enough. Go back to Stage 1.
- **Explaining the surreal** — Don't. The surreal element exists. It doesn't need a reason.
- **Clever dialogue** — Fujimoto characters don't banter. They say awkward, emotionally naked things. Write true, not smart.
- **Overusing meta** — One meta moment per oneshot. Two and it becomes a gimmick.
- **Safe endings** — A Fujimoto ending doesn't resolve neatly. It releases tension into something quiet and strange.
